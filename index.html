What LedgerLayer is

The infrastructure layer
your wealth has been missing.

Most households at this level of complexity hold assets across trusts, companies, pensions, and investment accounts — each managed by a different adviser, tracked in a different system, reported in a different format.

LedgerLayer is the authoritative record that sits above all of them. It consolidates positions, surfaces obligations, controls adviser permissions, and gives you a complete, structured view of your household's financial life.

It is not a portfolio tracker. It is not accounting software. It is not a dashboard bolted onto a bank account.

It is the system of record that wealth at scale has always required — and that, until now, simply did not exist as a product.

The problem

Complexity
without structure.

As wealth grows, the number of moving parts multiplies. Most households manage this with a collection of spreadsheets, email threads, and quarterly PDF reports from advisers who do not speak to each other.

The result is a picture that is always partial, often delayed, and impossible to act on with confidence.

01

No single source of truth

Assets held across institutions, each with its own reporting cadence and format. Consolidation is manual, slow, and error-prone.

02

Advisers operating in silos

Your solicitor, accountant, wealth manager, and IFA each hold a fragment of the picture. Coordination between them falls to you.

03

Obligations tracked by memory

Tax deadlines, trust reviews, and mortgage renewals live in inboxes rather than a system that surfaces them in advance.

04

Permissions managed informally

Deciding who can see what happens by email, with no audit trail and no consistency.

Why existing tools fall short

Built for a different
kind of household.

Consumer finance apps are designed for simplicity. Institutional platforms are designed for institutions. Neither is designed for the reality of a complex household — multiple entities, delegated authority, professional adviser relationships, and the need for control without being the one doing the administration.

LedgerLayer is designed specifically for that gap.

Help & guidance
Current view
You're on the household overview. This is your command centre — entities, people, obligations, and net worth all in one place.
Getting started
What is LedgerLayer? ›
LedgerLayer is a household wealth infrastructure platform. It consolidates your entities, people, advisers, and obligations into a single structured view.
How do I add an entity? ›
From the overview, find the Entities panel and click "+ Add entity". You can add companies, trusts, properties, SIPPs, ISAs, and more.
How do I invite an adviser? ›
Go to Advisers in the nav and click "+ Invite adviser". You can control exactly which entities each adviser can see and what level of access they have.
Obligations
What counts as an obligation? ›
Any deadline, filing, review, or renewal associated with a household entity. This includes tax returns, trust reviews, mortgage renewals, insurance renewals, and company filings.
Why is an obligation showing as "At risk"? ›
At risk means the due date has passed with no recorded action. Update the status or contact the responsible adviser to resolve it.
Security & access
Can advisers see everything? ›
No. Advisers only see the entities you explicitly grant them access to. You can set read-only or full access per entity, and revoke at any time from the Advisers directory.
Is my data encrypted? ›
Yes. All data is encrypted at rest and in transit. Access events are logged with timestamps. You can view the full audit log in the Timeline view.
Keyboard & navigation
What keyboard shortcuts are available? ›
Press ? anywhere in the app to see all shortcuts. Key ones: ⌘K for search, G then O for overview, N to add an obligation.
