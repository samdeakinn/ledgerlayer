What LedgerLayer is

The infrastructure layer
your wealth has been missing.

Most households at this level of complexity hold assets across trusts, companies, pensions, and investment accounts — each managed by a different adviser, tracked in a different system, reported in a different format.

LedgerLayer is the authoritative record that sits above all of them. It consolidates positions, surfaces obligations, controls adviser permissions, and gives you — and the people you choose — a complete, structured view of your household's financial life.

It is not a portfolio tracker. It is not accounting software. It is not a dashboard bolted onto a bank account.

It is the system of record that wealth at scale has always required — and that, until now, simply did not exist as a product.

The problem

Complexity
without structure.

As wealth grows, the number of moving parts multiplies. Most households manage this with a collection of spreadsheets, email threads, and quarterly PDF reports from advisers who do not speak to each other.

The result is a picture that is always partial, often delayed, and impossible to act on with confidence.

01

No single source of truth

Assets are held across institutions, each with its own reporting cadence and format. Consolidation is manual, slow, and error-prone.

02

Advisers operating in silos

Your solicitor, accountant, wealth manager, and IFA each hold a fragment of the picture. Coordination between them falls to you.

03

Obligations tracked by memory

Tax deadlines, trust reviews, and mortgage renewals live in inboxes or calendars rather than a system that surfaces them in advance.

04

Permissions managed informally

Deciding who can see what — and acting when that changes — happens by email, with no audit trail and no consistency.

Why existing tools fall short

Built for a different
kind of household.

Consumer finance apps are designed for simplicity. Institutional platforms are designed for institutions. Neither is designed for the reality of a complex household — multiple entities, delegated authority, professional adviser relationships, and the need for control without being the one doing the administration.

LedgerLayer is designed specifically for that gap.

How it works

From fragmented
to structured, in four steps.

1

Map your household

Define your household's structure: the individuals, entities, trusts, companies, and accounts that make up your financial life. This becomes the foundation everything else is organised around.

2

Connect your positions

Link accounts and holdings to the correct entity. LedgerLayer aggregates across institutions and gives you a consolidated view at both the entity and household level.

3

Assign access

Give each adviser, family member, or executor precisely the visibility they need. Access is role-based, logged, and revocable — with no ambiguity about who can see what.

4

Stay informed

Obligations, deadlines, and portfolio changes surface automatically. You spend less time assembling information and more time acting on it.

What LedgerLayer
holds together.

Tax & obligations

Capital gains, income tax, trust distributions, and filing deadlines are tracked against your actual positions. Nothing is missed because no one was looking.

Legal structure tracking

Trusts, limited companies, SIPPs, and offshore structures each sit in the right place within your household map, with key documents attached and review dates visible.

Adviser permissions

Each professional sees only what their role requires. Access is granted explicitly, scoped precisely, and withdrawn cleanly — with a full log of who viewed what and when.

Household entity view

See your entire household — every person, entity, and account — in a single structured view. Drill into any entity or surface the aggregate. Both are one click away.

Consolidated reporting

Produce a consistent view of household net worth, asset allocation, and income across all structures — formatted for your use or shared directly with advisers.

Succession readiness

Maintain a document vault with wills, trust deeds, powers of attorney, and beneficiary designations — accessible to the right people at the right time, including when it matters most.
